# ADR 0003: ML Forecasting Strategy and Operational Inference Constraints

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-22
- Owner: Lead Orchestrator & ML Lead

## Context
A central product requirement of HeatSense is that displayed forecasts (+24h and +48h air temperature) must be generated by HeatSense's own trained ML models. External weather forecasts (e.g., GFS, ECMWF IFS, Open-Meteo) must not be secretly substituted or smuggled in as future input features.

However, atmospheric temperature is driven by time-varying radiative forcing (solar radiation, cloud cover), synoptic wind advection, pressure changes, and precipitation. At inference time $t$, observations for $t + 12\text{h}$, $t + 24\text{h}$, and $t + 48\text{h}$ do not exist yet. Only information up to time $t$ (historical lags, rolling statistics, cyclic time encodings, static geographic features) is known.

## Options considered
1. **Recursive 1-step autoregressive forecasting ($t \to t+1 \to \dots \to t+48$)**:
   - Predict $t+1$ temperature, feed it back into lag features, and roll forward 48 steps.
   - Tradeoff: Without future exogenous radiation and cloud forcing, errors compound rapidly, and the model tends to either collapse to climatology or drift to unphysical temperatures by step 48.
2. **Direct multi-horizon regression models (Separate models for $t+24\text{h}$ and $t+48\text{h}$)**:
   - Train dedicated estimators $M_{24}$ and $M_{48}$ mapping state at time $t$ to $T(t+24)$ and $T(t+48)$.
   - Inputs strictly available at $t$: Lags of temperature ($t, t-1, t-3, t-6, t-12, t-24$), 24h rolling max/min/mean, solar hour cyclic encoding $(\sin, \cos)$ for target time, day of year cyclic encoding, latitude, longitude, and elevation.
   - Tradeoff: Avoids error accumulation of recursive loops. Naturally models the strong diurnal cycle (since target time of day is known exactly at $t+24$ and $t+48$, matching the same hour of day as $t$).
3. **Smuggling external numerical weather forecasts as inputs**:
   - Use GFS or ECMWF forecast temperature at $t+24$ as a feature to "predict" temperature.
   - Tradeoff: Strictly violates project rules (docs/01_PRD.md and Orchestrator instructions). Rejected.

## Decision
Adopt **Option 2 (Direct Multi-Horizon Modeling)** as the primary operational strategy:
1. Train distinct models for Horizon +24h and Horizon +48h.
2. Target definition:
   - For horizon 24h: $Y_{24} = T(t + 24\text{h})$
   - For horizon 48h: $Y_{48} = T(t + 48\text{h})$
3. Feature set strictly limited to initialization time $t$:
   - Historical temperature lags: $T(t), T(t-1), T(t-3), T(t-6), T(t-12), T(t-24)$
   - Historical diurnal range: $T_{\max}(t-24\dots t) - T_{\min}(t-24\dots t)$
   - Target solar/calendar encodings: $\sin(2\pi \cdot \text{hour}/24)$, $\cos(2\pi \cdot \text{hour}/24)$, $\sin(2\pi \cdot \text{doy}/365)$, $\cos(2\pi \cdot \text{doy}/365)$
   - Static spatial features: latitude, longitude, elevation
4. Candidate architectures:
   - Baseline: Persistence ($T(t)$) and Diurnal Persistence ($T(t - 24\text{h})$ or 7-day diurnal mean)
   - Random Forest Regressor
   - XGBoost Regressor
5. Strict evaluation:
   - Chronological test split (no random row shuffling).
   - Compute MAE, RMSE, and bias across all grid cells and on extreme heat days ($T > 38^\circ\text{C}$).
   - If skill over baseline is insufficient for operational confidence, the model card and UI must transparently state the limitation rather than inflating performance claims.

## Consequences
- Guaranteed zero future data leakage.
- Completely deployable in production using only recent past observations and astronomical calendar features.
- Honest, transparent evaluation comparing HeatSense models directly against diurnal baselines.

## Evidence
In diurnal meteorology, temperature at $t+24$ shares the exact solar hour as $t$. Direct regressors exploiting 24h lags and diurnal cycles consistently outperform multi-step unguided autoregressive rollouts by avoiding compounding error drift.

## Revisit conditions
Revisit if deep learning sequence architectures (e.g. Temporal Fusion Transformers or Physics-Informed Neural Networks) or owner-approved non-forecast exogenous drivers (e.g., satellite soil moisture feeds) become available.
